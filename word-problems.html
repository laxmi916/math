Math Word Problems

Learn addition, subtraction, multiplication and division. Practise one real-life word problem at a time.

Addition — putting amounts together

Use addition when two or more amounts are joined to find the total.

Word problem: Anu has 12 crayons. Her friend gives her 8 more crayons. How many crayons does Anu have now?

12 + 8 = 20

Answer: Anu has 20 crayons.

Subtraction — taking away or finding the difference

Use subtraction when an amount is taken away or when you need to find how many more or fewer.

Word problem: A shop has 35 balloons. It sells 9 balloons. How many balloons are left?

35 − 9 = 26

Answer: There are 26 balloons left.

Multiplication — equal groups

Multiplication is a quick way to add equal groups. Find the number of groups and the number in each group.

Word problem: There are 4 trays with 6 cupcakes on each tray. How many cupcakes are there altogether?

6 + 6 + 6 + 6 = 24

4 × 6 = 24

Answer: There are 24 cupcakes.
Tip: Phrases such as “each”, “every”, and “equal groups” may suggest multiplication. Read the whole story before choosing.

Division — sharing equally or making groups

Division finds how much each group receives or how many equal groups can be made.

Sharing equally

18 biscuits are shared equally among 3 children. How many does each child get?

18 ÷ 3 = 6

Answer: 6 biscuits each.
Making groups

There are 24 pencils. Each box holds 6 pencils. How many boxes are needed?

24 ÷ 6 = 4

Answer: 4 boxes.
Connection: Multiplication and division undo each other. Since 4 × 6 = 24, then 24 ÷ 6 = 4 and 24 ÷ 4 = 6.

How to solve any word problem

  1. Read the story carefully.
  2. Identify what you know and what you need to find.
  3. Choose addition, subtraction, multiplication or division.
  4. Write the number sentence and calculate.
  5. Check that your answer makes sense and include the unit.
